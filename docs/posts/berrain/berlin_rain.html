How rainy and cold is Berlin really?

berlin
weather
Published

September 6, 2026

I hear a lot of complaints about the weather in Berlin. These come from both other Germans as well as foreigners who both often argue that it is constantly cold and rainy in Berlin. In my personal perception foreigners tend to complain even a bit more although this varies a lot with individual preferences and the weather conditions in the place which people take as their point of reference.

In my personal perception, however, it hardly ever rains in Berlin in comparison to the places I lived in before such as Gothenburg in Sweden and Bielefeld in the western part of Germany. In addition, I personally do not mind rainy or cold weather as I can prepare for both with the right clothing. Some of my best holidays I had in the arctic region where I fully enjoyed hiking in snow-covered landscapes at air temperatures from -5 to -15° Celsius over several hours. In contrast, I really do not like summers in Berlin as it is constantly too hot for me and I find it more difficult to deal with heat than with cold.

Thus, my perception of the weather in Berlin clashes a bit with that of many other people. Therefore, the motivation for this article is to provide some objective foundation for the discussions about the weather in Berlin. Of course, perceptions about what weather conditions people prefer are subjective. However, the actual amount of precipitation is not. But let us check what the precipitation and the temperatures in Berlin really look like in a European comparison.

Four other European cities

So it is clear that the weather in Berlin constitutes the focus of this article. But we also want to know where the weather of Berlin stands in relation to other places in Europe. Thus, we need some places for a comparison. As I lived in Gothenburg before, I decided to include this Swedish city in my comparison. I remember the weather in Gothenburg to be more pleasant than the weather in Berlin as it is generally a bit colder and hence also summers are more comfortable and rarely too hot. But we will see later in this article if my memories are accurate or just a romanticization of my life in Sweden.

As my girlfriend comes from Croatia, I decided to include the Croatian capital of Zagreb in the comparison. She constantly claims that the weather in Croatia is much sunnier and warmer than in Berlin. However, I am not so sure about this and want to contrast our perceptions with empirical data.

In addition to Berlin, Gothenburg, and Zagreb, I decided to include Dublin in Ireland and Malaga in Spain in the comparison. I chose Dublin because when visiting Ireland a few years ago I really got the impression that the climate there is very different from the one in Germany. In particular the vegetation with palm trees growing in peoples’ garden and at the beach made me feel that the winters in Ireland must be much milder than in Germany. Finally, I chose Malaga because I wanted decided to include a city in Southern Europe in the comparison and Malaga is one of the southernmost cities in Spain.

For each of these five cities I found a weather station providing the data that I needed for a comparison of the weather conditions. You can browse the following map to see the actual location of these weather stations:

The locations of the five weather stations

The weather stations are nicely distributed over different parts of Europe with Dublin and Berlin being almost on the same latitude, while Dublin and Malaga are almost on the same longitude and also Gothenburg, Berlin, and Zagreb are almost on the same longitude.

Data collection and preparation

To carry out the desired analyses I needed data on precipitation and air temperature at daily resolution. Collecting the data was fairly straightforward for the German, the Irish, and the Swedish case. The German Weather Service maintains a nice open data portal that is very well documented in German and English. It provides recent and historical data from over 1,000 weather stations in Germany. I used the R package RDWD to access the data of the station “Berlin-Tempelhof” which I pass by many times in everyday life.

Also the Irish Meteorological Service (MET eirann) provides a nice open data portal with data from weather stations all over the Republic of Ireland. I downloaded data from the Glasnevin station in Dublin. The open data portal of the Swedish Meteorological Service (SMHI) is only available in Swedish. I downloaded the data from the weather station Göteborg A which is centrally located in Gothenburg.

In Spain there are different regional meteorological services that maintain weather stations in different parts of the country. The weather stations in and around Malaga are maintained by Red de Informacion Agroclimatica de Andalucia. Their data can be accessed via the R package Meteospain. I used the data from the station with the ID 29-1 which is located just outside of Malaga.

The Croatian Meteorological Service does not provide any open access to historical data from its weather stations. However, their website provides a contact form which I filled out and submitted. Two days later I received a file containing all the data for the city of Zagreb which I asked for. So their service is pretty good. I got data for the station Zagreb Maksimir.

After data collection, I decided to compare the weather in the five cities based on the daily amount of precipitation and the maximum daily air temperature in the 26 years from 01.01.2000 - 31.12.2025. The time span of 26 years feels large enough to even out the potential impact of outliers, while at the same time focusing on relatively recent data that should adequately capture the usual presented day weather conditions in the five cities. Precipitation is measured in milimetres (mm) and denotes the entire amount of rain that came down at the location of the weather station on a particular day. The maximum daily air temperature is measured in degrees Celsius (° C) and outlines the highest temperature recorded at a weather station within the 24 hours of a particular day. Of course, temperatures and rainfall can vary heavily within different parts of one and the same city. Therefore, many researchers work on methods to adequately expand point measurements of weather conditions to wider areas taking into account the build environment and many other factors. However, these methods come with inaccuracies, too, and also data from them is not widely available, yet, for the five cities of interest in this article. Hence I decided to keep it simple by assuming that the measurements of the weather stations adequately represent the overall weather conditions of the city they are located in.

After checking the data I realized that I will have to exclude a few years from the further analysis for some cities due to too many missing values. For the city of Malaga the years 2000 and 2001 were completely excluded from the analysis due to too many missing values. For the city of Gothenburg the year 2013 was excluded for the analysis of the temperature due to too many missing values. However, there is enough data on precipitation in 2013 in Gothenburg which was kept in the data set for the analyses on rain. In the case of Dublin the year 2020 was excluded due to too many missing values.

How hot or cold is Berlin in comparison?

Let’s first take a closer look at the temperature data for the five cities. For this purpose I have calculated the mean of the maximum daily temperature for each day of the year from 01.01.2000 - 31.12.2025 excluding only February 29th in leap years. The result is the following line chart:

Average maximum daily temperature from 2000 to 2025

The chart illustrates a couple of interesting things. First and maybe most obvious it shows that Malaga plays a bit in its own league as throughout the year the maximum daily air temperature in Malaga is much higher than in any of the other four cities. A closer look at the chart also reveals that the curves for Malaga and Dublin, although being far apart, seem to exhibit similar slopes. Also the curves of Berlin, Gothenburg, and Zagreb appear to follow a similar pattern with all 3 cities having colder winters and warmer summers than Dublin. Indeed, a look at the difference between the smallest and the highest average maximum daily temperature for each city supports our impression:

Difference between the smallest and the highest average maximum daily temperature
Berlin:  25.8
Dublin:  13.788
Gothenburg:  21.768
Malaga:  18.9125

Thus, the data shows Berlin, Gothenburg, and Zagreb have more distinct seasons with regard to the temperature than Dublin and Malaga. In addition, the average maximum daily temperatures on winter days in Berlin, Gothenburg, and Zagreb appear to be relatively similar, while the average maximum daily temperatures on summer days in these three cities seem to be farther apart.

To take a closer look at this, we can calculate the average maximum daily temperature per month for the five cities:

Average maximum daily temperature per month from 2000 to 2025

Indeed the average maximum daily temperature on a day in December is 6.13°C in Zagreb, 4.93°C in Berlin, and 4.18°C in Gothenburg, while the average maximum daily temperature on a day in July is 28.77°C in Zagreb, 25.42°C in Berlin, and 21.89°C in Gothenburg. Thus, temperature-wise these three cities have similar winters but rather different summers. With all three cities being almost on the same Longitude, one could either argue that Gothenburg experiences rather mild winters for a city that far in the north of Europe or that Zagreb has surprisingly cold winters with regard to it being roughly 2,000km south of Gothenburg or 1,000km south of Berlin.

Coming back to our focus on Berlin both of the line charts above show that Berlin’s average daily maximum temperature over the summer month is right in the middle of the five cities. Berlin in summer is clearly hotter than Dublin and Gothenburg but also clearly colder than Zagreb and Malaga. However, as only Gothenburg experiences lower average daily maximum temperatures than Berlin in winter, we can definitely say Berlin winters are on the colder side in the comparison of these five cities. In spring and autumn the average daily maximum temperatures of Berlin are, just like its geographical position, right in the middle between Zagreb and Gothenburg. As already said, Malaga generally plays a bit in its own league but in comparison to Dublin, the average daily maximum temperatures of Berlin are relatively comparable in spring and autumn with Berlin overtaking Dublin sometime in spring during the transition from winter to summer, while again falling below the average daily maximum temperatures of Dublin sometime in autumn on the way towards winter.

After this general overview of temperature development throughout the year, I would like to have a closer look at the rather warm and hot days of the year. While many people argue that it hardly ever gets warm in Berlin, other complain about the very hot summers of the city. So let’s check out how things actually stand in comparison to the other four cities. First, let’s have a look at the number of summer days per year. These are defined as days on which the maximum temperature reached at least 25°C.

Summer days

Again Berlin seems to be right in the middle of Gothenburg and Zagreb when it comes to the number of summer days per year. Malaga is far above with considerably more summer days than the other four cities, while Dublin is at the other end of the scale without a single summer day in many years. In fact, as the following bar chart demonstrates, Malaga has on average 165 summer days per year which constitutes almost exactly three times as many summer days per year as Berlin with 54.

Average number of summer days per year from 2000 to 2025

Thus almost half of the days each year in Malaga are days with a maximum air temperature of at least 25°C. In contrast, with an average of 96 summer days per year a bit more than every four day in Zagreb is a summer day, while roughly every seventh day in Berlin is a summer day. If the summer days were evenly distributed throughout the entire year, the residents of Berlin would experience one summer day per week. This is not little, in particular in comparison to the 17 summer days of Gothenburg and the 4 summer days of Dublin per year.

A look at the heat days which are defined as days on which the maximum daily air temperature reached at least 30°C illustrates a similar pattern.

Number of heat days per year

While the residents of Dublin have not experienced a single heat day from day from 2000 to 2025 (lucky them!), Malaga has considerably more heat day per year than all the other four cities with Berlin again being right between Zagreb and Gothenburg. In fact, Malaga on average experiences 81 heat days per year which really is a lot. However, the average number of heat days per year for Berlin is 14 which is exactly two weeks and thus probably much more than what many people would have thought.

Average number of heat days per year from 2000 to 2025

Thus, temperature-wise Berlin seems to be somewhere in the middle of the five cities considered, in particular during the summer months. Let’s now take a look at the precipitation data.

How rainy or dry is Berlin in comparison?

This line chart illustrates the overall amount of precipitation for each city and year from 2000 to 2025:

Total precipitation per year

While it may be difficult to identify specific patterns in this chart, it seems that Malaga in most years constitutes the city with the lowest amount of precipitation, while either Gothenburg or Zagreb in most years constitute the city with the highest amount of precipitation. To make the comparison a bit easier, I also plotted the average amount of precipitation per year for each city.

Average sum of precipitation per year from 2000 to 2025

Berlin with an average amount of 564mm of precipitation per year is clearly on the dryer side of the five cities with only Malaga having less precipitation per year with 454mm. In contrast, Dublin with 757mm, Zagreb with 879mm, and Gothenburg with 942mm have considerable more precipitation per year than Berlin. Thus, Berlin, and this might be a surprise for many people, is much closer to Malaga when it comes to the overall amount of precipitation per year than to Dublin, Gothenburg, and Zagreb.

However, the overall amount of precipitation per year does not tell us how many rainy days a city experiences per year. After all the total amount of precipitation might come down over just a couple of days with heavy rain or it might be evenly distributed throughout the year with many little sprinkles each day. Therefore, I also decided to plot the number of days with at least 1mm of precipitation for each city.

Number of days with rain per year

While Malaga still clearly has the lowest numbers of days with rain in each year, either Dublin or Gothenburg have the highest number of days with rain. Zagreb and Berlin appear to have a very similar number of days with rain per year which is a bit surprising considering the much higher overall amount of precipitation per year in Zagreb. Yet, the following bar chart confirms that Berlin with on average 103 days with rain per year and Zagreb with on average 95 days of rain per year are relatively close together in comparison to the other three cities.

Average number of days with rain per year from 2000 to 2025

Thus it looks like it generally rains more in Zagreb than in Berlin but that this higher amount of precipitation comes down on fewer days. To check whether this conclusion holds true, I plotted the number of days with heavy rain which are defined as days with at least 20mm of precipitation. The line chart below clearly shows that Zagreb in many years constitutes the city with the highest number of days with heavy rain.

Number of days with heavy rain per year

In contrast, Berlin seems to be the city with the lowest number of days with heavy rain in most years. Let’s look at the average number of days with heavy rain per year to check whether this impression is correct.

Average number of days with heavy rain per year from 2000 to 2025

Indeed, Berlin on average only has 3 days with heavy rain per year which is less than any of the other four cities. Particularly surprising maybe is that even Malaga on average has double as many days with heavy rain per year as Berlin. In fact, with its 6 days with heavy rain per year on average Malaga also ranks higher than Dublin which on average has 5 days of heavy rain.

Zagreb, however, is the unbeaten champion in this category with on average 11 days with heavy rain per year. This is almost four times as many as Berlin! With regard to Zagreb also being the city with the largest difference in average maximum daily air temperatures in summer and winter, it looks a bit like the climate in Zagreb in general is more extreme than in the other four cities with relatively cold winters, relatively hot summers, lots of total precipitation per years, and many days with heavy rain.

Conclusion

So what can we say about the weather in Berlin. Well, in terms of rain it seems to be rather on the dryer side, at least in comparison to Dublin, Gothenburg, Malaga, and Zagreb. With regard to temperature Berlin ranks relatively central among the other four cities. While its winters are colder than those in Malaga, Dublin, and Zagreb, it still on average experiences 54 summer days and 14 heat days per years which make its summers considerably warmer and hotter compared to the summers of Gothenburg and Dublin.

Keeping these results in mind, I still feel comfortable complaining about the too hot summers in Berlin. To many others, who complain about too cold winters and too much rain, I would like to say: There is no such thing as bad weather, only bad clothing!
